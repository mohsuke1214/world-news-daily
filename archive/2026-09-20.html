The new lines of southern Lebanon

国連平和維持軍の装甲車両がレバノン南部の事実上の国境に沿ってゆっくりと移動しているのは、地形や砲撃やドローンの脅威のためではなく、1マイル進むごとに戦争によって大きく変貌した風景を再学習することを意味するからだ。

続きを読む

UNIFIL（国連レバノン暫定軍）は、最初のレバノン占領後のイスラエル軍の撤退を検証するために創設された1978年以来、この地域を監視してきた。

国連は20年前、UNIFILの任務を拡大し、イスラエルとレバノンのイランが支援するヒズボラとの間の戦闘における停戦順守の監視を含めた。ヒズボラはイスラエルの二度目の侵攻に対抗するために1982年に設立された過激派組織である。

それからほぼ半世紀が経ち、平和維持軍の任務は年​​末に期限切れとなり、米国とイスラエルの反対により更新されない。未だに永続的な停戦は成立していない。

Original (English)

French UNIFIL peacekeepers patrol beyond the "yellow line" — the boundary of occupying Israeli forces — through the flattened towns of Houla, Kfar Kila, and Odaisseh. Israel has established a restricted buffer zone beyond that line after a U.S.-brokered ceasefire this year. Diego Ibarra Sánchez for NPR hide caption

The U.N. peacekeepers' armored vehicle moves slowly along the de facto border in southern Lebanon, not because of the terrain or the threat of shelling and drones, but because every mile means relearning a landscape profoundly transformed by war.

UNIFIL — the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon — has been monitoring this area since 1978, when it was created to verify Israel's troop withdrawal after its first occupation of Lebanon.

The U.N. expanded UNIFIL's mandate 20 years ago to include monitoring adherence to a ceasefire in fighting between Israel and Lebanon's Iran-backed Hezbollah - the militant group created in 1982 to counter a second Israeli invasion.

Almost half a century later, the peacekeeping force's mandate will expire at the end of the year and due to U.S. and Israeli opposition, will not be renewed. There is still no durable ceasefire.

続きを読む

Original (English)

Pavadairayan, 60, a migrant laborer known as a "basket man," carries fish in a basket on his head from boats to the auction house at Kasimedu Fishing Harbour in Chennai, India. Viraj Nayar hide caption

He works from 2 a.m. to 7 a.m. every day. In pitch darkness, he carries baskets of fish on his head from boats in Kasimedu harbor to the auction house, navigating slimy and damp pathways littered with fish scales and wet with seawater. It's a third of a mile each way. He makes the trip 40 or 50 times during his shift for the daily auction, which begins at 4 a.m.

Pavadairayan, who is 60 years old and goes by one name, is one of hundreds of workers known as basket men. It's a tough job. Each basket weighs about 35 pounds. Their bosses don't trust them: "If supervisors think that fish are missing, they can dock your pay and fine you several thousands of rupees. Basket men have few rights," he says.

Despite the hardships, Pavadairayan says he loves the job because the rest of the day is his own. He spends his mornings sleeping and lounging.
