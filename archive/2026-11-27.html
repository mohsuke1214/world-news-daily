11月20日、同国の国会は麻薬密売、16歳未満に対する強姦、人道に対する罪など6つの犯罪に対する死刑を廃止する改正刑法を承認した。

続きを読む

この改革は、ベトナムの与党共産党が政治的反対派や独立した市民社会に対する弾圧を強化している中で行われたことを考えると、特に顕著である。

Original (English)

Vietnam , a country once considered one of the world's most prolific executioners, is steadily reducing the number of crimes punishable by death.

On November 20, the country's National Assembly approved a revised Penal Code that abolishes capital punishment for six offenses, including drug trafficking, rape of a person under the age of 16 and crimes against humanity.

When the legislation takes effect next March, just four offences — treason, murder, terrorism and illegal drug production — will remain punishable by death, down from 44 in 1997.

Although Hanoi has given little indication that it intends to do away with capital punishment entirely, few countries that still regularly execute prisoners have narrowed its legal application so considerably.

The reforms are particularly striking given that they come as Vietnam's ruling Communist Party has intensified its crackdown on political dissent and independent civil society .
