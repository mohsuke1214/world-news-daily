Original (English)

Five European countries have announced a plan to send rejected asylum seekers to a processing centre in Rwanda as early as next year, drawing condemnation from rights groups who said it would leave vulnerable people at risk of abuse.

The planned scheme, which is still being negotiated with Rwanda, would send people to a centre where the migrants could choose to return home, settle or go somewhere else where they had a right to stay, Kigali said on Monday.

Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands said that the plan aimed to deter more people from migrating to Europe. The European Union has worked for years to tighten migration rules, reduce the number of asylum seekers arriving and increase deportations of migrants who are not entitled to stay.

“I think this will be a game changer, making us able to confront the massive challenges with illegal migration,” Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said. “We will break down the business model of human smugglers. We will take back control of our borders and we will remove the incentives for illegal migration.”

Five EU states announce plans for immigration ‘return hubs’ in Rwanda

ドイツ、オーストリア、オランダ、デンマーク、ギリシャは「重大な道徳的欠陥」として批判される政策への挑戦に直面する可能性が高い

EU加盟5カ国は、欧州に滞在する法的権利を持たない人々のためにルワンダにオフショアセンターを設立する計画を発表したが、試験計画では法的な問題に直面することが予想され、「重大な道徳的欠陥」として非難されている。

続きを読む

ドイツ、オーストリア、オランダ、デンマーク、ギリシャの５カ国は、欧州からの追放に対してあらゆる訴えを尽くした人々を第三国に送還することを認めるＥＵの新たに合意された返還法を初めて利用しようとしている。

月曜日に発表された声明の中でEU5カ国は「実施に向けてパートナー国ルワンダとの具体的な交渉が始まる」と述べ、この計画は「不法移民のさらなる抑制、密航業者のビジネスモデルの解体、そして国民の負担の軽減に重要な貢献」となるだろうと述べた。

Original (English)

Germany, Austria, Netherlands, Denmark and Greece likely to face challenges to policy criticised as ‘grave moral failing’

Five EU member states have announced plans to set up an offshore centre in Rwanda for people with no legal right to stay in Europe, in a pilot scheme that is expected to face legal challenges and has been denounced as “a grave moral failing”.

The five countries – Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Denmark and Greece – are the first to seek to use the EU’s newly agreed returns law that allows people who have exhausted all avenues of appeal against their removal from Europe to be sent to a third country.

In a statement released on Monday the five EU countries said “concrete negotiations with partner country Rwanda will begin for implementation”, saying the scheme would make “an important contribution to further curbing illegal migration, dismantling the business model of smugglers and relieving the burden on our population”.

Officials hope to send 100 people to Rwanda from early next year to test the model. The scheme will apply to adults whose claims to asylum have been rejected or others with no legal right to live in the EU.
