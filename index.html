彼の組織は、操作的なオンライン行為に対する EU 規則の強化を推進しました。同氏は、この提案は消費者を日々イライラさせている問題に取り組むための良い出発点であると考えている。

Original (English)

Under the European Commission's proposed Digital Fairness Act (DFA), a company would have to remind you before the free trial ends and get your explicit agreement before it can start charging you. The proposed law would also make it easier to cancel a subscription online and require reminders before certain contracts renew automatically.

The Commission presented the proposal on Wednesday. It wants to give consumers more control over how much time and money they spend online .

"We haven't seen that kind of reform in consumer protection in the last 20 years," Agustin Reyna, director general of the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC), told DW.

His organization pushed for stronger EU rules against manipulative online practices . He sees the proposal as a good starting point for tackling problems that frustrate consumers every day.

The proposal also includes a ban on manipulative marketing tactics, so-called "dark patterns." A concrete example: You are trying to reserve a hotel room and the website claims that only three are left, a potentially false claim to pressure you into buying.
